## Research
Understanding how the brain sense and decide how to navigate in environment is a fundamental goal of neuroscience. Of the senses, touch may be the most vital. Touch signals is generated by a diverse array of sensory neurons, and i am interested in understanding how they are used in the downstream brain circuitry. By combing precise tactile stimuli and behavioral tracking with modern neurophysiological recording methods (including calcium imaging and multielectrode array electrophysiology), as well as computational and engineering tools, I believe we can gain insights into circuit-level principles: <b><em> How do neuron in central somatosensory system respond across variety of contexts in health and disease?</em></b>  To that end, my research has spanned three main directions:

1. <b>Mechanistic</b>: In the somatosensory system, features such as the direction of a moving touch are represented by activity in the Primary Somatosensory Cortex (S1). But does S1 merely encode this information, or is it actually required for animals to perceive it? 
<a href="https://www.abstractsonline.com/pp8/#!/20433/presentation/17347" target="_blank">[Yuna Lee, <b>Ruorong Qi</b>, Alan Emanuel; <b>SfN 2024</b> — PSTR277.01/C159]</a>.

2. <b>Descriptive</b>: Fine sensorimotor control is severely impaired or absent when tactile signaling is disrupted. Although the motor symptoms of Parkinson’s disease are extensively studied, the neural mechanisms underlying the tactile deficits reported by over 90% of patients remain poorly understood. Specifically, does dopamine depletion in S1 contribute to altered perception of gentle touch in Parkinson’s disease? 
<br>

3. <b>Normative</b>: Somatosensory circuits may be optimized to encode tactile information efficiently, how would a Parkinsonian circuit deviate from it?

Together, these efforts aim to advance our understanding of signal processing in the somatosensory system. Doing so has the potential to lead to treatments for sensorimotor disorders, whether through improvement of brain-machine interfaces or development of neurobiological interventions.
